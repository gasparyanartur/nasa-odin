# Martian Map resource catalogue

Date: 2026-11-04 (first built 2026-11-03). Built only from the challenge brief, the README, 12 lane reports, 6 check reports (a to f), 1 injection screen, and a later Opus review. No link was re-opened for this document. Where a check and a lane disagree, the check is used (check-b's `elev` field name was wrong; check-d read the files). Check-e (sites, links, services) and check-f (suit and safety documents) are the newest sources and outrank every earlier source here. Every number is marked Moon, Earth or Mars where the source says which. This is a reading list: it holds no code and no build plan, because the FAQ says teams may not begin work before the event.

## 1. Bottom line

- **Richest places:** Gale with Mount Sharp and Jezero both score 8 of 9. InSight in Elysium Planitia is next at 5 of 9. Everywhere else scores 2 or lower.
- **Gale's edge:** it has a 1 m terrain model, and it is the only surface site with measured radiation dose. **Jezero's edge:** ground-radar data. Its confirmed terrain is 20 m. The weather record is not an edge: REMS at Gale is also listed as ongoing.
- **The trade-off:** the best ice sites (Deuteronilus Mensae, Arcadia Planitia) score 2 and 1. No site in the files has both dense data and ice evidence.
- **Easiest in a browser:** 14 endpoints passed the browser access tests in total: 12 in check-d (list there) plus 2 terrain files tested on 2026-11-04 (check-e). Each returned HTTP 200 and `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`. They are: Mars Trek tiles (NASA's map tile service, no key), `HiRISE_Global` tiles (an Esri host), two large map files that can be read in pieces, all four rover path files, both raw image feeds, one USGS STAC collection endpoint, DONKI, and the Gale 1 m terrain model and Jezero 20 m terrain mosaic (both redirect from planetarymaps.usgs.gov to an S3 host, with `Accept-Ranges: bytes`). Pixel values were not tested, so whether the MOLA file holds raw heights is still open.
- **Newer facts (check-e, check-f):** Perseverance weather (MEDA) derived data is confirmed to sol 1859. Gale (Mars surface) dose is now sourced: Hassler 2014 gives 0.210 +/- 0.040 mGy/day. A Moon-oriented NASA suit standard (Rev C) was read: 14 hours total in the suit (a note, not surface-specific). The Special Regions limit is now -18 C (255 K) and water activity above 0.60 (Mars), replacing the older -25 C and 0.5.
- **Also easy:** DONKI (NASA's space weather event database) returned JSON with no key on its new address. The rover path files are about 0.6 to 1.7 MB each and run to sol 2022 for Perseverance and sol 5045 for Curiosity (a sol is a Mars day); the Curiosity traverse file carries no sol.
- **Elevation trap:** Trek's elevation and slope layers are rendered pictures, without raw height values. The 200 m global terrain model has no piece-readable version, as of a 2024 USGS post. It is an 11 GB plain file.
- **Missing:** no Mars number exists in the files for suited walking speed, suited slope limit, walkback distance or pressurized rover slope. The numbers found are Moon or Earth. There is also no gridded dose map, and no confirmed rock map finer than 60 km with global coverage (a thermal-inertia rock proxy from TES appears in a search snippet only).
- **Unsettled until Nov 13:** official resources, the judging guide and the free offers. Licence limits on MCD (a French Mars climate model), HRSC (a European orbiter camera), SWIM (buried ice maps) and Cesium need care. See section 4.

## 2. Location table

Terms used from here on:
- **MRO** (NASA orbiter): CTX (context camera), HiRISE (sharpest camera), CRISM (mineral spectrometer), SHARAD (ground-penetrating radar), MCS (atmosphere sounder), MARCI (daily weather camera).
- **MGS** (Mars Global Surveyor, older NASA orbiter): MOLA (laser altimeter), TES (thermal spectrometer), MOC (camera).
- **Mars Odyssey** (NASA orbiter): THEMIS (infrared and visible camera), GRS (gamma ray and neutron suite, reads elements and hydrogen), MARIE (radiation instrument).
- **Mars Express** (ESA orbiter): HRSC (stereo camera), OMEGA (mineral spectrometer), MARSIS (low-frequency radar).
- **MAVEN** (NASA orbiter): SEP (energetic particle sensor).
- **Curiosity, also MSL** (rover at Gale): RAD (radiation detector), REMS (weather station), APXS, ChemCam, CheMin, SAM, DAN (chemistry and mineral instruments), Mastcam (camera).
- **Perseverance, also Mars 2020 or M20** (rover at Jezero): MEDA (weather station, with TIRS, its infrared sensor), RIMFAX (ground radar), PIXL, SHERLOC, SuperCam (chemistry instruments), Mastcam-Z and Navcam (cameras). Ingenuity was its helicopter.
- **Spirit and Opportunity, also MER:** rovers at Gusev and Meridiani. **InSight:** lander with SEIS (seismometer) and TWINS (wind and air temperature).
- **Data words:** DEM or DTM (terrain model), ppd (pixels per degree), PDS (NASA's Planetary Data System archive), ODE (search service over PDS orbital data), WMTS (map tile service standard), STAC (searchable catalogue standard for map files), COG (large map file readable in pieces over the web), EVA (spacewalk or surface walk), EZ (Exploration Zone, a proposed human landing area).

Global layers that reach every site and are not scored:
- Optical: CTX 5.0 m/px, 99.5% of Mars.
- Terrain: 200 m MOLA-HRSC blend.
- Minerals: OMEGA 1.48 km, TES about 15 km, CRISM survey ~200 m (180 m in a news snippet).
- Thermal: THEMIS about 100 m.
- Atmosphere: MCD model, Montabone dust maps.
- Radiation: DONKI events, OLTARIS.
- Ice: GRS in 2, 5 and 10 degree bins.

Scoring rule: one point per column where the files name downloadable or queryable data specific to that site (on-site measurement, or a site or regional product). Global layers, model presets, viewer-only items and search-snippet claims score 0.

How the rule was applied:
- Material that originates from NASA or a US government body (USGS, PDS nodes, NTRS) is outside the non-NASA clause. It stays, with a citation. Origin is judged by the mission, not by the host: data from a NASA mission instrument counts as NASA origin also when the instrument team serves it from its own site (msss.com for MOC and MARCI, asu.edu for THEMIS and TES, uahirise.org for HiRISE, sharad.psi.edu for SHARAD, mastcamz.asu.edu). This reading is an inference from the legal page wording "does not originate from NASA"; no page defines it. A research product derived from mission data by an outside group (SWIM, for example) is not mission data and is judged as non-NASA.
- Three catalogue rows are split by layer, on purpose: the Trek imagery row (NASA-hosted layers KEEP, the Esri-hosted `HiRISE_Global` DROP), the Trek elevation row (MOLA-only layers under NASA origin, blend layers under open licence), and HRSC (only the USGS-hosted blend is KEEP, HRSC taken direct from ESA is HOLD).
- Non-NASA material stays only if it is free and public without restriction, or carries an open licence. A non-commercial clause, an account wall or a missing licence fails that test.
- The rule covers what goes into the submitted project (data, code, images, text). A paper read for one number is a citation, not project material. It goes in the References field. Its figures and text are not copied.
- The legal page also exempts "other space agency partners".
