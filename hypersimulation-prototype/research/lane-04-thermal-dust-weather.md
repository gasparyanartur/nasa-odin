Richest locations:
- Gale crater (Curiosity): REMS covers sol 1 to 4488 (2012-2025) at 1 Hz, with hourly 5-min samples (pdsatmospheres REMS page). It is the longest surface record and the only one with ground T.
- Jezero (Perseverance): MEDA runs from Feb 2021 to ongoing, with 5 air-T detectors, TIRS, wind, humidity, pressure and dust sensors, on the live-updated PDS bundle. It is the best choice for "now".
- InSight, Elysium Planitia: TWINS plus pressure at 0.1-2 Hz for 1188 sols, but the record has an early-2021 gap.
- Viking 1 (Chryse) and Viking 2 (Utopia): only 1976-79 data, with a ~1-hour sol grid. Good for seasonal cycle and baselines.
- Presets in MCD: Gale, Jezero, InSight, Viking 1 and 2, Phoenix, Pathfinder, Spirit, Opportunity, Zhurong. This makes these sites the easiest to cross-check against measurements.
- Dust-storm hotspots seen in MARCI weekly reports: the Acidalia storm track and Chasma Boreale/north polar cap edge (MSSS 2017, 2018 pages). Not tied to an "astronaut walk" site.

Best for a 2-day build:
1. MCD web interface, and offline MCD if registration is granted fast. It is the only entry that gives predicted conditions for a chosen place, season and local time, global, with dust scenarios. Caveat: no documented HTTP API. The web page is "moderate use only", so pre-compute a lookup grid offline or screenshot-free-export from the form.
2. THEMIS 100 m day/night IR mosaics plus the TES 20 ppd thermal inertia map. Static global rasters in simple cylindrical projection, easy to tile as a layer. They show where the ground is warm or cold and rocky or dusty. TES PNG is 7200x3600 and simple to load. Cite: Putzig 2005.
3. Montabone dust climatology (CC BY-SA 3.0, netCDF, ~50 MB per Martian year, 3x3 deg kriged). It is the only openly licensed dust layer, with clear terms and small files. Gives a seasonal dust-opacity overlay and a storm-risk layer.
Runner-up: MEDA and REMS CSVs to validate the MCD predictions at Jezero and Gale.
